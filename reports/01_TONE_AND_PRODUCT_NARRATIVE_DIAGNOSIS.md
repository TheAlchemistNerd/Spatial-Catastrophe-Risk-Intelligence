# Tone and Product-Narrative Diagnosis

**Project:** Spatial Catastrophe Risk Intelligence  
**Author:** Nevil Maloba  
**Purpose:** Preserve the editorial diagnosis that redirected the project from a policy framework towards a product-led Insurtech white paper.

## Author's concern

The earlier edition sounded like a policy and governance framework. It attempted to cover many institutional questions simultaneously and became detached from the original crowd-intelligence, AI, YOLO, wildfire, and actuarial-loss workshop.

The intended project is an Insurtech product comparable in narrative ambition to *Mwendo Pamoja Continuous Underwriting*. Governance, regulation, privacy, and institutional controls should support that product story rather than dominate it.

The project uses the Kenyan and British spelling **Actuarial modelling**.

The paper must also tell the livelihood story of every catastrophe. For flood, that means explaining Kenyan river basins and showing how a physical event becomes household, agricultural, infrastructure, insurance, and fiscal loss. Drought, wildfire and rangeland fire, locust infestation, landslide, severe storm, and extreme heat require equivalent human and economic treatment.

The contents should remain selective. The reference paper does not burden the reader with a catalogue of small sections. The parts need enough space to carry a book-like argument while avoiding unnecessary length or repetition.

## Tone diagnosis

The earlier voice was institutional, defensive, and policy-led. It sounded like a regulatory implementation framework whose principal subject was governance rather than an Insurtech product whose value unfolds through people, catastrophe events, actuarial modelling, and insurer decisions.

The benchmark uses a founder-led, commercially argumentative, book-like voice. Its recurring narrative rhythm is:

> Human problem -> financial mechanism -> information failure -> product architecture -> mathematics -> operating decision -> capital consequence -> control boundary.

It introduces the affected person's working and financial reality before introducing equations. Controls support the product story instead of becoming the story.

## Product-centred editorial direction

The working proposition is **Spatial Catastrophe Risk Intelligence (SCRI)**: an insurer-facing continuous catastrophe-underwriting and loss-intelligence capability.

The narrative should proceed through:

1. A recognisable Kenyan livelihood or insurer problem.
2. The physical and economic catastrophe mechanism.
3. The information failure in conventional insurance practice.
4. The crowd, AI, earth-observation, and sensor intervention.
5. The actuarial transformation from hazard to loss.
6. The underwriting, claims, accumulation, reinsurance, or finance decision.
7. The limits, uncertainty, and institutional boundary.

## Required livelihood journeys

### Flood

Follow households, farms, roads, traders, public services, and insured portfolios through Kenya's basin systems. The lower Nzoia plains should be contrasted with the Tana, Athi, and Ewaso Ng'iro systems rather than treating flood as a single national process.

### Drought

Follow pastoral and agricultural livelihoods through rainfall deficits, water access, vegetation condition, livestock body condition, market deterioration, distress sales, and cumulative household loss.

### Wildfire and rangeland fire

Explain ignition, detection, fuel, wind, suppression, ecological function, water-tower exposure, livestock and tourism effects, and the detection-to-severity hypothesis.

### Locust infestation

Follow swarm movement, lifecycle stage, crop stage, pasture exposure, surveillance lead time, control intervention, and agricultural income loss.

### Landslide

Ground the narrative in local slope conditions, rainfall, access disruption, road blockage, settlement vulnerability, and the limits of national susceptibility maps.

### Severe storm and extreme heat

Show consequences through working hours, health stress, cooling demand, crop and livestock effects, power interruption, transport disruption, and business downtime.

## Editorial boundaries

- The project is an Insurtech product thesis, not a general national disaster-policy framework.
- The California workshop supplies hypotheses and design ideas, not Kenyan evidence.
- The actuarial charter is a quality guide, not the project's subject matter.
- The Mwendo Pamoja paper controls narrative ambition, integration, and reading experience, not catastrophe content.
- Model output must remain distinct from contractual authority, claims authority, public-warning authority, and regulatory approval.
- No benefit should be described as certain before it has been tested against a baseline and a credible counterfactual.

## Desired voice

The final voice should be confident but calibrated, technically serious but readable, commercially motivated but not promotional, and human before mathematical. It should prefer specific mechanisms and evidence over adjectives such as "revolutionary", "flawless", "absolute", or "unprecedented".
