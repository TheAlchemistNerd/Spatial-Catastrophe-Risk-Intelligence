## Primary project description

I originated Spatial Catastrophe Risk Intelligence (SCRI), a Kenya-first Insurtech and catastrophe-intelligence product being developed by Philtechent LTD. SCRI is designed as a nationwide risk operating system that connects community intelligence, earth observation, drones, cameras, environmental sensors, institutional records and artificial intelligence into timely, georeferenced and traceable catastrophe evidence.

Its machine-perception workbench combines the best validated capabilities of Ultralytics, vision transformers and specialist geospatial models for detection, segmentation, tracking, classification, oriented-object analysis, depth-related evidence and open-vocabulary discovery. Probabilistic inference and hazard-specific science estimate how flood, drought, wildfire, locust and crop-pest infestation, landslide, severe storm and extreme heat evolve across time and place.

Actuarial modelling translates those changing hazard states through exposure, vulnerability, response capacity and financial terms into distributions of physical, insured, uninsured, claims-reserve, economic-capital, fiscal and investment loss. The resulting intelligence can support accessible community warnings and action, portfolio monitoring, continuous underwriting, claims triage and nowcasting, reinsurance, bank credit-risk analysis, infrastructure resilience, disaster-risk financing and climate-investment monitoring, reporting and verification.

SCRI's nationwide architecture combines one governed national evidence spine with locally calibrated hazard engines, county and community observation networks, and secure institutional workspaces. Communities participate as knowledge holders, observers, beneficiaries and resilience-investment partners, with accessible reporting, feedback, consent, redress and benefit pathways designed into the product.

The commercial model extends beyond SaaS: enterprise licences, catastrophe data and model licences, API services, managed event intelligence, portfolio and claims analytics, resilience-project appraisal and MRV, implementation services, and partnerships with licensed insurers and financial institutions. Institutional revenue can support dependable national infrastructure and expand the public-benefit layer.

The current research and publication programme includes the six-part *Kenya Multi-Hazard Catastrophe Intelligence* manuscript series, the LinkedIn articles *From Catastrophe Intelligence to Investable Resilience* and *Beyond SaaS*, and a proposed Kenya Bankers Association study on whether spatial flood and drought intelligence can improve bank credit-risk identification.
